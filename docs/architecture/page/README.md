# The architecture page

The source of A2MArchitecture, the interactive page that the owner reads the design from. It is published as an artifact outside this repository; these files are what it is built from.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `arch.json` | The data: every part, its place in a view, its text, its state, its arrows and what each carries, and the walks through each view |
| `outline.json` | The top level: five stations on one line, the route a call takes, and three shelves beneath it, each naming the parts of the root view it holds, with one sentence of its own; and the seam, the one part drawn as the boundary between the kernel and the packs |
| `template.html` | The page: a workspace of three panels. The middle is the Module Map, a container and component drawing: each station and shelf is a container, its parts are modules nested inside it, and a module that holds parts shows their count and opens in place into a container of its own. The Provider Contract is drawn as a seam beneath the kernel with its extension points on it, and the packs hang off it as plug-in modules of one shape, above the target systems; the shelves lie beneath as layers. Between containers one link is drawn per pair, with its count and its strongest kind; a module's own links show when it is hovered or chosen, or when its container is opened. The left panel holds search, the layers (a tree of the stations and every view, nested as the views nest), filters by state and by kind of link that fade what they leave out, and the calls to follow. Any layer or walk can be opened alone as a process map: the rest fades away and its parts are laid out along the flow, numbered by step. The right panel reads the chosen part in depth: where it sits, its text and state, what it receives and hands over, what is inside, the steps and walks it takes part in, what it never does, its decisions linked to their records, and its open questions. The hover card stays for a glance. Both side panels fold to a rail, and the page remembers that and the chosen layer. Under 1000 pixels the left panel is a drawer and the right panel a sheet from the bottom. The map loads one script, d3 7.9.0 from cdnjs.cloudflare.com; the panels work without it. The earlier Line view is gone: the layers and the reading panel took over its reading |
| `build.py` | Checks the data, the outline and the template, and sets the data into the template: `python3 build.py <output file>` from this folder. It fails when a view names a part that does not exist, when a part of the root view is in no station or in two, when a part cannot be reached from the first screen, when there are more than six stations, when an arrow does not join two different parts, when a step of a walk lights an arrow its view does not have, when the seam is not in exactly one station with a station after it or has no row of extension points, when a part cites a decision number with no record, or when the template lacks a panel, the title, the dark colour rules, or loads a script from elsewhere than the allowed hosts at an exact version |
| `proposed-names.md` | Names coined for the page that no other document uses yet ([decision 0301](../../decisions/0301-every-part-has-a-proper-name.md)) |

Rules for keeping it true:

- A decision that changes the design changes this data in the same step, under the part it changes, with the alternative that lost.
- The title bar shows the whole build's percentage complete, counted by `tools/progress.py` from the roadmap when the page is built. The page is rebuilt and republished whenever a package is marked done.
- Every part has a proper name (decision 0301), and every arrow says what passes along it. The reading panel lists a part's arrows under "What it receives and hands over"; the map draws them between modules, gathered into one link per pair of containers until a module is chosen.
- The first screen stays short. A reader sees the containers and their modules, folded; text appears in the reading panel when they choose something. Add detail to a part, never to the first screen.
- The state of a part (built, partly built, not built) follows the crate design and the roadmap. Many inner parts were given their state from the documents and not from the code; where the two differ, the code is right and the data is corrected.
- The text was drafted from the design documents by a worker and is not yet checked line by line. It describes; it does not rule. Where it disagrees with a specification section or a crate design, that document is right.
