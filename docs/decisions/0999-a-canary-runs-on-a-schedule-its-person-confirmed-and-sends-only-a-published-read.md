# 0999. A canary runs on a schedule its person confirmed, and sends only a published read

**Status:** Accepted. Design decision. The owner had not read this record when it was merged.

**Date:** 2026-11-25

**Builds** package 9.1b, canaries on a schedule of their own, apart from a watch, which [decision 0390](0390-the-open-questions-of-2026-11-24-are-settled-by-the-lead-and-the-first-release-is-what-progress-counts-toward.md) made a row and [decision 0397](0397-what-the-first-release-needs-of-the-packages-left-and-what-waits-until-after-it.md) held until it had a design of its own: "a canary nobody asked for sends requests to a person's system that nobody confirmed". This record is that design. It **keeps** decision 0380's marks, their refusal, and their lift, and decision 0396's marked listing, unchanged: a canary is one more run of the job kind `canary`, and what its drift and its pass do is theirs. It **keeps** the watch as it is; a watch's check is still a canary of the read it watches.

**Changes** the scheduler (a new table, `canaries`, owned by `a2m-sched`, migration 0030; `Scheduler::ask_canary`, `confirm_canary`, `due_canaries`, `next_canary_due`, `submit_canary`, `end_canary_check`, `end_canary`; four failure codes; a canary's check held to the reserve and the share of a watch's check at run time; a watch's confirmation counts the canaries on its surface); the registry (`canary.create`, `canary.confirm`, `canary.get`, `canary.list`, `canary.end`, none listed); the kernel's tending; the saved API description and both generated clients; spec sections [217](../spec/2-kernel/217-traces-evidence-and-repair.md) and [403](../spec/4-building-tools/403-testing-and-publish-gates.md); the [action catalog](../reference/action-catalog.md), the [ledger schema](../reference/ledger-schema.md), [file formats](../reference/file-formats.md); the designs of [`a2m-sched`](../architecture/crates/a2m-sched.md) and [`a2m-actions`](../architecture/crates/a2m-actions.md); [SCOPE.md](../../SCOPE.md); the roadmap (9.1b done).

## Plan, stated before the build

The question the row's caution asks has two halves. **Who confirms a canary's schedule**, so that no request goes to a person's system that they did not agree to; and **what a canary may send**, so that what they agreed to is all that goes. The slice answers both in the scheduler, where the schedule lives, and proves them through the kernel against the test application with a screen changed on purpose, and through the product's commands in a real browser against `targets/example-app` with its form changed by `POST /__control/screens`.

The documented design weighed against: spec section 403 said "scheduled read-only canaries re-run the contract gate, more often for platforms that ship interface changes frequently", and the file formats said a binding's `drift_risk` "sets how often canaries run". Read that way a canary starts by itself when a version is published, at a rate the target's author chose. That is the canary nobody asked for, and it is not built.

## Decision

1. **A canary is its own row, apart from a watch.** One published read, with an input fixed when it is asked for, sent at a fixed interval until an end, to find whether the knowledge the read goes through still fits the system. It keeps nothing of what it read and compares nothing: what it leaves are drift marks, kept by its drift and lifted by its pass (decision 0380), and on its row how many checks it made, how many due times it missed, and what its last check found (`passed`, `drifted`, `failed`, or `rested`, with the failure, never a value). After a drift it goes on at its interval, so that a pass after the system comes back lifts the mark; a watch pauses instead, because a person reads its changes.

2. **Who confirms it: the person it acts for, by an act of their own, on the engine's page.** Anyone who may ask for a watch may ask for a canary (`canary.create`, a viewer's action). The canary is kept `asked`, and sends nothing. Only the person it acts for, whose session its reads use, confirms it, with presence, on the engine's page (`canary.confirm`, a person's act that an agent's call is sent to the page for), exactly as a watch's schedule is confirmed ([decision 0283](0283-watches-reach-an-agent-through-the-registry-and-their-person-confirms-each-on-the-page.md)). The page shows the read, its input, the interval, the end, how many checks and requests that is against the surface's rate, and that a drift stops every tool relying on the same knowledge. The scheduler holds the rule: a confirmation by anyone else is answered as no such canary, and `due_canaries` plans confirmed canaries alone. Nothing else makes or confirms a canary: not a publish, not a setting, not a target's `drift_risk`, not an agent. A canary nobody confirmed ends at its end, having sent nothing, and cannot be confirmed after.

3. **What it may send: one published read, as published when it was asked.** The operation must be a read (`canary_not_a_read` otherwise). Its version is the one published when it was asked; its confirmation is refused under any other (`canary_version_changed`), and a canary whose target has another published version ends, `version_changed`, in the pass that sees it, before another check is admitted: a new version may send other requests, which nobody confirmed. Each check is a run of that read, for that person, with that input, of the job kind `canary`, which the engine refuses for any step that writes ([decision 0380](0380-a-drift-marks-the-knowledge-it-was-seen-in-and-stops-every-binding-that-relies-on-it.md)), and the scheduler refuses a check that writes before anything is kept. A run that rests, waiting for a sign-in or stopped by a drift, is let go: a canary asks nothing of anyone.

4. **How much it may send: a watch's limits, shared with the watches.** The interval's floor, the longest it lasts, and as many per person as watches, from the same settings, with the codes of a watch for the first two and `canary_too_many` for the third. A check is `batch` work, leaves the reserve of every budget to the caller's own reads, and spends at run time the share of its surface that watches spend. Its confirmation must fit, with the watches and the other canaries on its surface, in that share, and a watch's confirmation now counts the canaries on its surface too. Due times are a watch's: the moment of confirmation, plus a phase from its identifier, plus whole intervals; a due time missed is counted and never checked late, and one check follows for the latest.

5. **It ends** at its end, when another version is published, or by `canary.end`, from its person or the caller that asked for it. A check under way is let go, and its run cancelled when the check opened it.

## Choices

- **A person's confirmation, not the target's author's or the publisher's.** The alternative weighed was canaries declared in the target's files, at the rate `drift_risk` sets, started on publish and confirmed by the person who presses publish. It was not taken: the publisher confirms a version, not a stream of requests under someone's session for days, and a target's files are knowledge about the system, written by a builder who may be an agent. `drift_risk` is not read by a canary; it could one day suggest an interval on the confirmation page.
- **Its own row, not a watch with a flag.** The alternative weighed was a watch that records no changes. It was not taken: a watch is the ledger's row, joined to the ledger's rules of checks, changes, and baselines that a canary has no use for, and it pauses on a drift, where a canary goes on. The canary's row is the scheduler's own, so its check's share and reserve at run time are found without the ledger.
- **A canary ends with its version.** The alternative weighed was a canary that follows each new version, so that it vouches for a repair. It was not taken: the repaired version may send other requests, and a repair that changed the knowledge is not stopped by the old mark (decision 0380) anyway.

## Not built

A canary's MCP resource, as a watch has. A canary shown in `engine.status` among the engine's health. An interval suggested from `drift_risk`. A canary of a pipeline. The `drifting` sign (decision 0323), which a canary's check would carry when the engine reports it.

## What the work showed

- A canary needed nothing of the engine: the job kind `canary`, the drift's mark, and the pass's lift were all there from package 9.1. What was missing was a schedule a person had agreed to.
- A drift stops the canary's own run as `stopped`, not `failed`. Left resting, the next check was handed that same run by ledger rule 6 and found the old drift again; the check now lets a resting run go, so each check is a run of its own.
