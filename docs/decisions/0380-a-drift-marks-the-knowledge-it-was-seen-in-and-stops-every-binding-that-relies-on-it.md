# 0380. A drift marks the knowledge it was seen in, and stops every binding that relies on it

**Status:** Accepted. Design decision, made under [decision 0294](0294-the-owner-hands-the-open-questions-to-the-agents.md). The owner had not read this record when it was merged.

**Date:** 2026-11-24

**Changes:** what the engine says of a binding's health (`EngineEvent::BindingHealth` gains `knowledge`; a new module, `drift.rs`, of `a2m-engine`); which jobs drift stops (`Run::admits`); what `a2m-actions` keeps of a drift (a new table, `actions_drift_marks`, migration `0028`) and how its runner fills `Conditions::drifted`; section 217, "Drift and the repair loop"; the designs of [`a2m-engine`](../architecture/crates/a2m-engine.md) and [`a2m-actions`](../architecture/crates/a2m-actions.md); the [ledger schema](../reference/ledger-schema.md). The first slice of package 9.1.

## Context

Section 217 says that when a canary or a run reports `drift`, "the affected tools are marked unavailable", and section 403 that a failed canary marks the binding unhealthy "before anyone runs a write". Neither was built. The engine already passed over a binding named in `Conditions::drifted`, and reported a drift as `EngineEvent::BindingHealth`, but the control plane kept that only as a run event ("the health of a binding is the engine's own table, which is not built"), and the runner never filled `drifted`. A canary that found a changed screen therefore stopped nothing: the next write through that screen was sent, and failed at its first locator, or worse.

Three questions had no answer. Which tools a drift affects, when a canary is a read and the tools that matter are writes. What a mark is held to, so that a repair lifts it and nothing else does. And whether a canary is itself stopped by the mark it is meant to check.

## Decision

1. **A drift names the knowledge it was seen in.** When a step fails with `drift`, the engine reports the pack entry the step refers to (`Step::refers_to`): in the web pack a screen, in another pack the entry of its kind. The kernel reads nothing of what an entry means; it holds the entry by its name and by the digest of the whole pack section that holds it (`section_digest`). A drift seen outside a step, or at a step that refers to no entry, names the binding itself, by its digest (`binding_digest`). This is `DriftKnowledge`, carried in `EngineEvent::BindingHealth::knowledge`.
2. **A mark stops every binding that relies on the same knowledge, through the same mode, on the same surface.** `drift_covers` lists every binding of a plan, by operation and mode, with a step that refers to the marked entry in a section of the marked digest. A mark of a whole binding, where nothing says which knowledge drifted, stops every binding of its mode on its surface: unknown means stop. So a canary's read of an order that finds the order screen changed stops the write of a line item through the same screen, because knowledge lives in one place (rule 8) and both name it.
3. **A mark is held to the knowledge, not to the version.** It is kept per target, and applies to any version that holds the same section digest (or binding digest). A repair, a version whose screen model was changed to fit what the system shows, has another digest and is not stopped. A version that changed something else, a description, another file kind, is stopped all the same. Keying the mark by the plan's hash was rejected: any publish, even of the unchanged draft under a new number, would have lifted it.
4. **A write the mark stops is refused before anything is sent.** The runner reads the marks of the run's target into `Conditions::drifted` when it fills each job; the engine's choosing refuses the binding, `drift`, `binding_drifted`, naming the operation and the mode, before the preview's read, the freshness check, or the save. Nobody is asked to approve it, and its run is `stopped`, not failed, so the same run resumed after the mark is lifted continues: it is validated again and previewed, by the state rule of [the record of the same number on resuming a run stopped before anything was planned](0381-a-run-stopped-before-anything-was-planned-is-validated-again-when-resumed.md). The first build of this slice showed the gap: the ledger refused the resumed preview as `ledger_state_unexpected`. A mark that cannot be read stops every binding of the plan; a store that cannot answer ends the job before the engine runs.
5. **A canary is never stopped by a drift, and its pass lifts what it vouches for.** A job of the kind `Canary` writes nothing (the engine refuses a canary of a writing tool), so drift does not pass over its bindings: it is the check of whether they fit again. When its read passes through a binding, the engine reports the binding healthy with every entry its steps refer to and the binding's own digest, and the control lifts the marks of exactly those, through that mode, on that surface. Today the canary that exists is a watch's check ([decision 0283](0283-watches-reach-an-agent-through-the-registry-and-their-person-confirms-each-on-the-page.md)): a read run as `Canary`.
6. **The mark and the run event are written in one synced transaction**, so a drift that was seen is never lost before the next job is filled.

*Instead of:* marking a whole surface on every drift. It is simplest and safe, but one changed screen would stop every write to the application, the repair of one screen file would have to lift marks it says nothing about, and the engine would throw away the one fact it has, which step failed. It remains the rule where that fact is missing. *Instead of:* marking only the `(operation, mode)` that drifted, as `Conditions::drifted` is shaped. A read canary would then never stop a write, which is the case section 403 is written for.

## What this does not do yet

- A tool stopped by a mark is not shown as unavailable in `tool.list` or in the engine's health; it is refused when called. Section 604 names drift alerts in the health view.
- A write's own drift (at its preview or before its save) stops the run but leaves no mark: only a read reports `BindingHealth` today.
- `drifting` (a field matched by a locator that is not the first of its list) is not reported: it needs the pack to say which locator matched.
- Canaries on a schedule of their own (section 403, "after publishing") are not built: a watch is the canary.
- The repair bundle of section 217, step 2, and the pack's repair proposer (decision 0323, hypothesis H-49) are not built. Here the repair is a version a person or an agent authors and publishes; nothing heals in a run (rule 1).
- The browser-level proof needs a screen of the test application that can be changed on purpose (see the roadmap's row 9.1).

## Proof

`crates/a2m-engine/tests/drift.rs`: a canary whose read drifts names the screen; the mark covers the read and the write through that screen and not a write that does not; the write is refused `binding_drifted` with no step sent; a canary is not stopped by drifted conditions while a live read is, and its pass vouches for the screen and the binding; a mark does not stop a version whose screen changed and stops one that kept it; a drift no step named stops every binding of its mode on its surface until that binding changes. `crates/a2m-actions/src/tests/drift.rs`, through the kernel against the test application, with the test pack's screens changed on purpose (`Hooks::screen_shows`): a watch's check catches the change and pauses as `drifted`; the write through the same screen is refused, nobody is asked to approve it, and the application heard nothing of the record; a repaired version is published and the write is approved, made once, and stored as sent; and, in the second test, a version that kept the screen stays stopped until a canary passes again, after which the run the mark stopped is resumed and rests for its approval.
